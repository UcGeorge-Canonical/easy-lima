**CODEBASE-01 — Major — Diagnostics must remain useful when Lima, storage, or part of the registry is unavailable**

- **Dimension:** Observability & Operational SRE Debuggability.
- **Disposition:** Implementation-design requirement.
- **Grounding:** Frozen [FEATURES.md:86](/Volumes/Nebula/dev/Canonical/local/.egm/reviews/2026-11-18-v1/inputs/FEATURES.md:86) requires actual paths, failed-step identification, and distinguishable missing-drive and failed-SSH cases. In the prototype, [local.py:281](/Volumes/Nebula/dev/Canonical/local/.local-launcher/local.py:281) makes instance listing depend on successful Lima/platform and storage checks; [local.py:490](/Volumes/Nebula/dev/Canonical/local/.local-launcher/local.py:490) performs that listing before printing the instance/log paths; and [local.py:498](/Volumes/Nebula/dev/Canonical/local/.local-launcher/local.py:498) stops `doctor` at the first thrown prerequisite failure. [local.py:299](/Volumes/Nebula/dev/Canonical/local/.local-launcher/local.py:299) also loads every registry record without isolating malformed entries. The TUI catches an initial instance-listing failure by using an empty status map at [tui.py:75](/Volumes/Nebula/dev/Canonical/local/.local-launcher/tui.py:75), then defaults the selected instance to “Not created” at [tui.py:165](/Volumes/Nebula/dev/Canonical/local/.local-launcher/tui.py:165).
- **Operational risk:** The condition the user needs to diagnose can suppress the evidence needed to diagnose it. An inaccessible volume or unsupported Lima version is different from an absent instance; presenting those states alike undermines recovery and ownership decisions. One damaged record should not hide every other registered environment.
- **Concrete mitigation:** Separate locally known information from successfully observed runtime state. Inspection should report each check as passed, failed, unavailable, or skipped; show expected paths and known associations when readable; and preserve “unknown/unreachable” as distinct from “not created.” Isolate individual record errors. Keep diagnostic inspection separate from repairs and workload verification: the prototype’s `verify` and `git-check` dispatch through `up` at [local.py:517](/Volumes/Nebula/dev/Canonical/local/.local-launcher/local.py:517), which can provision or reconfigure before checking anything. Validate that missing storage, unavailable Lima, and one malformed record still produce useful, nonmutating diagnosis.

**CODEBASE-02 — Major — Resumability needs evidence across attempts, including an indeterminate state after interruption**

- **Dimension:** Observability & Operational SRE Debuggability.
- **Disposition:** Implementation-design requirement already implied by F3/F8.
- **Grounding:** Frozen [FEATURES.md:42](/Volumes/Nebula/dev/Canonical/local/.egm/reviews/2026-11-18-v1/inputs/FEATURES.md:42) requires recoverable incomplete setup and conflicting-operation locks; [FEATURES.md:86](/Volumes/Nebula/dev/Canonical/local/.egm/reviews/2026-11-18-v1/inputs/FEATURES.md:86) requires distinguishable completed/incomplete stages and actionable failure reports. The prototype records ownership before creation at [local.py:353](/Volumes/Nebula/dev/Canonical/local/.local-launcher/local.py:353), but the record contains project identity and resources, not an operation history. Provisioning uses one final fingerprint at [local.py:378](/Volumes/Nebula/dev/Canonical/local/.local-launcher/local.py:378), written only at [provision.sh:97](/Volumes/Nebula/dev/Canonical/local/.local-launcher/provision.sh:97). Every provisioning attempt appends to one raw guest log at [provision.sh:12](/Volumes/Nebula/dev/Canonical/local/.local-launcher/provision.sh:12). Host timeouts become a generic exception at [local.py:44](/Volumes/Nebula/dev/Canonical/local/.local-launcher/local.py:44), and the host lock is released in `finally` at [local.py:294](/Volumes/Nebula/dev/Canonical/local/.local-launcher/local.py:294).
- **Operational risk:** Preserving ownership is valuable, but it does not identify the last proven completed stage, distinguish successive retries in a log, or prove that guest work stopped when a host connection timed out. The inspected code contains no reconciliation of guest execution after that timeout. A retry can therefore encounter work whose outcome is unknown while the available evidence looks merely “incomplete.”
- **Concrete mitigation:** Carry a minimal local operation record through wrapper, Lima, and guest activity: operation/attempt identity, stable project/instance identity, stage, timestamps or elapsed time, result, and relevant log locations. Record the last proven completion and represent interrupted or unobserved guest work as indeterminate until reconciled. Before retrying, determine whether the previous attempt remains active and reuse its instance. Shipped stages can have documented retry behavior; arbitrary custom provisioning must retain F8’s explicit no-transactional-rollback limit. Exercise interruption separately during creation, provisioning, identity configuration, and final SSH validation.
  
  This calls for local correlation and usable evidence, not a distributed tracing service, remote telemetry backend, or dashboard.

**CODEBASE-03 — Major — Define the boundary of “logs avoid secrets” when accepting user provisioning and guest commands**

- **Dimension:** Observability & Operational SRE Debuggability.
- **Disposition:** Author decision needed; proposed concept clarification.
- **Grounding:** Frozen [FEATURES.md:50](/Volumes/Nebula/dev/Canonical/local/.egm/reviews/2026-11-18-v1/inputs/FEATURES.md:50) accepts user-provided Lima YAML, [FEATURES.md:68](/Volumes/Nebula/dev/Canonical/local/.egm/reviews/2026-11-18-v1/inputs/FEATURES.md:68) includes noninteractive guest execution, and [FEATURES.md:86](/Volumes/Nebula/dev/Canonical/local/.egm/reviews/2026-11-18-v1/inputs/FEATURES.md:86) says logs avoid secrets without distinguishing wrapper diagnostics from raw child output. The prototype’s general runner includes full arguments and captured error output in exceptions at [local.py:37](/Volumes/Nebula/dev/Canonical/local/.local-launcher/local.py:37); provisioning saves combined output at [provision.sh:12](/Volumes/Nebula/dev/Canonical/local/.local-launcher/provision.sh:12). Conversely, [git_identity.py:84](/Volumes/Nebula/dev/Canonical/local/.local-launcher/git_identity.py:84) deliberately suppresses secret-bearing key-management arguments and output.
- **Operational risk:** The existing secret-command wrapper is a useful protection for known sensitive operations. It does not establish a general guarantee for arbitrary template scripts or commands that themselves print credentials. Without a stated boundary, implementation could expand raw logging while believing the concept’s secret-avoidance requirement is already covered.
- **Concrete mitigation:** Clarify that wrapper-generated events and errors must omit private keys, passphrases, credential-bearing arguments, and unnecessary environment values. Decide explicitly how raw custom-script and command output is treated: whether it is persisted, how users are told it can contain sensitive material, and what access/retention limits apply. Avoid promising universal sanitization of arbitrary output. Preserve useful nonsensitive context—operation, stage, exit status, and next action—even when sensitive details are withheld.
  
  Suggested amendment: “Wrapper-generated diagnostics exclude known secrets. Raw output from user commands or provisioning is treated as potentially sensitive and is not automatically included in shareable diagnostics.” The author should approve the intended boundary; the implementation design can choose the mechanism.

**CODEBASE-04 — Major — Identity diagnostics must separate local agent, guest forwarding, signing, and GitHub transport failures**

- **Dimension:** Observability & Operational SRE Debuggability.
- **Disposition:** Implementation-design requirement.
- **Grounding:** Frozen [FEATURES.md:76](/Volumes/Nebula/dev/Canonical/local/.egm/reviews/2026-11-18-v1/inputs/FEATURES.md:76) promises selected identities, signing, and an expected-account check; [FEATURES.md:78](/Volumes/Nebula/dev/Canonical/local/.egm/reviews/2026-11-18-v1/inputs/FEATURES.md:78) requires clear failures without unrelated-agent fallback. In the prototype, [git_identity.py:50](/Volumes/Nebula/dev/Canonical/local/.local-launcher/git_identity.py:50) maps every nonzero `ssh-add` result to an empty key list. [git_identity.py:58](/Volumes/Nebula/dev/Canonical/local/.local-launcher/git_identity.py:58) then ends with a broad instruction to unlock Keychain and rerun setup. [git-check.py:48](/Volumes/Nebula/dev/Canonical/local/.local-launcher/git-check.py:48) recommends adding the public key whenever no successful GitHub greeting is parsed, although this also encompasses transport or host-key failures.
- **Operational risk:** Named profiles on two host platforms introduce more possible failure layers. An empty agent, inaccessible socket, locked credential store, incorrect forwarded key, signing failure, unreachable GitHub, and wrong authenticated account require different actions. A generic setup/key-registration recommendation can encourage unnecessary credential changes and make adoption harder to diagnose.
- **Concrete mitigation:** Preserve the existing exact-key, signing, repository-override, and expected-account checks, but report their layers separately. Show the selected profile and public fingerprint, host agent availability, guest-forwarding result, signing result, transport/authentication result, and account comparison. Treat an unavailable external service as unavailable, rather than proof that a key is unregistered. Keep offline signing diagnosis available and ensure profile-specific repair instructions never fall back to another agent or replace a preserved key automatically.
